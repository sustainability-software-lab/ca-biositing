### Executive Summary
The data quality audit of the `mv_biomass_fermentation` dataset reveals significant structural and metadata integrity issues that impact the traceability and reliability of the bioconversion yields. The most critical finding is the 100% loss of experimental context, with `experiment_id` and `experiment_date` fields entirely unpopulated across all 2,996 records. This prevents the analysis of temporal trends or batch-specific effects, which are vital for identifying calibration drift in reactor vessels or variations in microbial strain performance. Additionally, the presence of 123 duplicated rows and 126 non-unique `record_id` values indicates a failure in the data ingestion pipeline or entry process, potentially leading to skewed yield averages.

While core fermentation parameters such as titers and sugar concentrations are present, there is a notable inconsistency in unit reporting, with 246 records lacking specified units. While the domain context allows for unitless growth measures like `OD600TEOF`, the volume of missing units suggests that other quantitative metrics (e.g., sugar titers or conversion rates) may be ambiguous. Furthermore, the almost complete absence of qualitative notes (99.4% missing) limits our ability to validate outliers against known mechanical failures or contamination events. Addressing the missing experimental IDs and resolving record duplication should be the highest priorities for the data engineering team.
