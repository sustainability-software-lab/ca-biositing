### Executive Summary
The audit of the `mv_biomass_fermentation` dataset reveals a critical systemic failure in metadata retention and data integrity. Most alarmingly, 100% of the records lack experiment identifiers, dates, and analyst attributions. This total loss of traceability renders the bioconversion yields scientifically unverifiable, as there is no way to correlate specific fermentation runs with environmental conditions, equipment calibration, or the specific timeline of the research. The lack of `analyst_email` values is particularly concerning for troubleshooting the high variance expected in inoculum effects and yield calculations.

In addition to the metadata collapse, the dataset suffers from significant technical debt in its structure. Approximately 4% of the rows are exact duplicates, and there are 126 instances of non-unique `record_id`s, indicating a failure in the database's primary key enforcement. Furthermore, 8.2% of records are missing units, which, in a domain where 'g/L' and 'g/g substrate' are often used interchangeably, introduces a risk of order-of-magnitude errors in bioconversion efficiency reporting. Immediate remediation is required to re-link these records to their source experimental logs before the data can be used for modeling or publication.
