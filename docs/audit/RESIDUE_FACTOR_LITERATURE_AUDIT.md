## 1. Executive Summary & High-Priority Findings

This report presents a resource-by-resource audit of all **127 resources** in
`public.resource` (along with all **37 rows** in `public.residue_factor`, **94
rows** in `public.resource_availability`, and **18 active resources** currently
producing rows in `data_portal.mv_biomass_volume_estimate`) against
peer-reviewed literature, UC Davis Cooperative Extension Cost & Return Studies,
California Biomass Collaborative (CBC / LBNL) assessments, and USDA NASS / CDFA
County Agricultural Commissioner statistics, with specific focus on agricultural
and processing practices in **California and the North San Joaquin Valley
(NSJV)**.

### 1.1 Headline Findings (Verified Against Live `biocirv_db`)

1. **Broken Unit ID (`_acreage_unit_id = 18` `'bushels'` Instead of `19`
   `'acres'`) Suppresses All Acreage-Based Orchard Prunings & Field Residues
   (`Sub-Issue #1`)**:
   - In `mv_biomass_volume_estimate.py` (line 42), `_acreage_unit_id = 18` maps
     to `'bushels'` in `public.unit` (`1 = 'tons'`, `2 = 'week'`,
     `18 = 'bushels'`, `19 = 'acres'`). Because `_acreage_unit_id` is used in
     both **Path B** (`census_based_volumes`, line 170) and **Path D**
     (`acreage_based_volumes`, line 342), **every acreage-based residue stream
     returns 0 rows (`NULL`)** in the live materialized view:
     - `almond branches` (`id = 16`, `RF_ID = 1`, `prune_trim_yield = 1.5`):
       currently `0 rows`; changing `18 → 19` restores **`591,525.0 t` (2017)**
       and **`783,568.5 t` (2022)** (`522,379 ac × 1.5`).
     - `grape vines` (`id = 7`, `RF_ID = 15`, `prune_trim_yield = 1.1`):
       currently `0 rows`; changing `18 → 19` restores **`143,607.2 t` (2017)**
       and **`152,647.0 t` (2022)** (`138,770 ac × 1.1`).
     - `cotton stem mix` (`id = 6`, `RF_ID = 10`, `prune_trim_yield = 1.2`):
       currently `0 rows`; changing `18 → 19` restores **`57,319.2 t` (2017)**
       and **`39,981.6 t` (2022)** (`33,318 ac × 1.2`).
     - `walnut tree sticks` (`id = 15`, `RF_ID = 36`, `prune_trim_yield = 0.6`):
       currently `0 rows`; changing `18 → 19` restores **`64,130.4 t` (2017)**
       and **`63,851.4 t` (2022)** for San Joaquin (`72,248 ac`) + Stanislaus
       (`34,171 ac`). Adding `bnb_acres.bearing_acres` (`parameter_id = 5`) to
       the `COALESCE` at lines 361–363 recovers Merced's 2022 walnut acreage
       (`11,359 ac`, where `parameter_id = 7` is suppressed `(D)`), bringing
       2022 NSJV `walnut tree sticks` to **`70,666.8 t`** (`117,778 ac × 0.6`).

2. **Corn Stover Silage-vs-Grain Bug (`~43.5×` Overestimate) & Substream
   Double-Counting (`Sub-Issue #2`)**:
   - In **Path E** (`census_production_weight`, lines 388–455),
     `corn stover whole` (`id = 12`, `RF = 9`, `mid = 1.02`),
     `corn stover stalks` (`id = 26`, `RF = 11`, `mid = 0.571`),
     `corn stover cobs` (`id = 21`, `RF = 7`, `mid = 0.153`), and
     `corn stover husks` (`id = 33`, `RF = 8`, `mid = 0.082`) are linked to
     `primary_ag_product_id = 16` (`'corn - all'`) and joined to USDA Census
     `production` (`parameter_id = 1`) in **`tons` (`unit_id = 1`)**.
   - In USDA Census, **corn for silage** is reported in `tons` (`4,517,173 t` in
     2017; `5,216,315 t` in 2022 in NSJV), whereas **corn for grain** is
     reported in `bu` (`unit_id = 24`). Because corn silage harvests the entire
     above-ground plant for dairy feed, **it leaves zero stover**.
   - Meanwhile, `public.county_ag_report_record` already tracks `'corn - grain'`
     under **`primary_ag_product_id = 45`** (`106,243 t` in 2023; `122,305 t` in
     2024 across San Joaquin and Merced) separately from `'silage - corn'`
     (`primary_ag_product_id = 56`: `5,028,537 t` in 2023; `5,308,777 t` in
     2024). Re-linking the corn stover residue factors from `pap_id = 16` to
     `pap_id = 45` reduces NSJV `corn stover whole` from **`5,320,641.3 t`
     (2022)** to **`91,369 dry t` gross (`36,548 dry t` sustainable at 40%
     removal) in 2023** and **`105,182 dry t` gross (`42,073 dry t` sustainable)
     in 2024**.

3. **Walnut Kernel-vs-In-Shell Basis Mismatch (`~2.8×` Overestimate), Duplicate
   Fines Factor (`~75×`), & Merced Meats-Only Reporting (`Sub-Issue #3` &
   `#4`)**:
   - `walnut shells` (`id = 27`, `RF_ID = 35`) uses `factor_mid = 1.494`
     (`1.34–1.647`), which is a **shell-to-kernel** ratio
     (`~60% shell / 40% kernel = 1.50`), but Path A multiplies it by County Ag
     Report walnut tonnage (`242,318 t` in 2023; `182,324 t` in 2024), which is
     reported on an **in-shell** basis in San Joaquin (`160,000 t` /
     `111,000 t`) and Stanislaus (`66,334 t` / `56,009 t`), yielding
     **`362,023.1 t` (2023)** and **`272,392.1 t` (2024)**. True dry shell mass
     is **`0.54 dry t/t in-shell`** (`0.50–0.58`).
   - Worse, `walnut shelling fines` (`id = 8`, `RF_ID = 34`) was assigned the
     exact same `1.494` factor (`362,023.1 t` in 2023; `272,392.1 t` in 2024),
     whereas true shelling fines/dust represent only
     `0.01–0.03 dry t/t in-shell` and are a substream of shelling.
   - Conversely, in `county_ag_report_record`, Merced County (`06047`,
     `carr_id = 215, 218`) recorded **walnut meats only** (`15,984 t` in 2023;
     `15,315 t` in 2024 across `~29,500 ac` = `0.52 t/ac`) rather than in-shell
     weight (`~58,000–65,000 t`).

4. **Upstream `almond shells` 1:1 Meats Copy in `county_ag_report_record`
   (`carr_id = 14, 15, 18`) & `resource_production_record` Bugs
   (`Sub-Issue #3`)**:
   - In `public.county_ag_report_record`, `almond shells` (`pap_id = 37`,
     `carr_id = 13..18`) copied `almond meats` (`pap_id = 35`) 1:1 in **3 of 6
     county-year rows**: `carr_id = 14` (2023 SJ `110,200 t`), `carr_id = 15`
     (2023 Stanislaus `235,431 t`), and `carr_id = 18` (2024 Stanislaus
     `241,260 t`), while `carr_id = 13` (2023 Merced `120,743 t`),
     `carr_id = 16` (2024 Merced `91,194 t`), and `carr_id = 17` (2024 SJ
     `68,100 t`) report distinct shell tonnages (`2023 MV = 466,374 t`;
     `2024 MV = 400,554 t`).
   - Separately, `public.resource_production_record` (queried by
     `mv_biomass_search`) has 21 rows with `geoid = 'NSJV'` (which fail `Place`
     joins), 10× missing-zero typos on `rpr_id = 20` (2019 SJ hulls `18,000 t`
     vs. `180,000 t`) and `rpr_id = 40` (2024 SJ hulls `24,300 t` vs.
     `243,000 t`), and the same 3 meats-copied shell rows
     (`rpr_id = 68, 69, 72`).

5. **Wet-vs-Dry Conflation Across High-Moisture Pomaces & Vegetable Culls
   (`Sub-Issue #5`)**:
   - Path A multiplies fresh wet crop production by **wet-basis** residue
     generation rates (`tomato pomace` `0.04` at `75–80%` MC; `grape pomace`
     `0.25` at `55–65%` MC; `peach pomace` `0.24` at `80–85%` MC;
     `sweet potato culls` `0.406` at `70–75%` MC) and outputs the result
     directly as `dry_tons` (with `wet_tons = dry_tons`), inflating dry biomass
     availability by **`3.3×` to `7.4×`**.

### 3.2 Deep-Dive Technical Analysis — Batch 1

#### A. Almonds (`id=32, 5, 9, 16, 1, 108, 93, 100`)

- **Hulling & Shelling Mass Balance (_Aktas et al., 2015_; _Offeman et al.,
  2014_; _Almond Board of California, 2023_):** In the North San Joaquin Valley
  (Stanislaus, Merced, and San Joaquin counties together harvest $>420,000\text{
  bearing acres}$ of almonds), trees are mechanically shaken onto the orchard
  floor in August–October and solar-dried in windrows for $5\text{–}10\text{
  days}$ before sweeping and pickup. Field-run almonds arriving at the
  huller/sheller consist of:
  $$\text{Field-Run Weight} \approx 25\text{–}28\%\text{ Kernel} + 50\text{–}56\%\text{ Hull} + 15\text{–}20\%\text{ Shell} + 2\text{–}5\%\text{ Sticks/Dirt/Hash}$$
  Normalized per **$1.00\text{ ton}$ of marketable almond kernels (meats)** at
  $5\text{–}6\%$ moisture:
  - **`almond hulls` (`id=32`):** Generates **$2.05\text{–}2.50\text{ air-dry
    tons}$** at $10\text{–}13\%$ moisture, equal to **$1.80\text{–}2.20\text{
    bone-dry tons per ton of kernel}$ (`mid = 2.00`)**. _Nonpareil_ (which
    comprises $\sim 45\text{–}50\%$ of NSJV plantings) has a large, fleshy hull
    (`~2.1–2.3 dry t/t kernel`), whereas hard-shell pollinizers (_Monterey_,
    _Butte_, _Padre_, _Fritz_) have slightly lower hull-to-kernel ratios
    (`~1.7–2.0 dry t/t kernel`). Because almond hulls are a high-value
    commercial dairy feed in the NSJV ($\sim\$90\text{–}\$150/\text{ton}$, fed
    up to $15\text{–}20\%$ of lactating cow dry matter intake), County
    Agricultural Commissioners report `Almonds, Hulls` directly as a commodity
    line item. However, Path C (`commodity_direct`) passes this air-dry tonnage
    ($453,148\text{ t}$ in 2023; $539,587\text{ t}$ in 2024; $2\times$ inflated
    to $906,296\text{ t}$ and $1,079,174\text{ t}$ by duplicate `place` `geoid`
    rows) directly into `mv_biomass_volume_estimate` as `dry_tons` without
    deducting its $10\text{–}13\%$ moisture content (`~11% MC`, i.e., $\times
    0.89$).
  - **`almond shells` (`id=5`):** Generates **$0.50\text{–}0.80\text{ bone-dry
    tons per ton of kernel}$ (`mid = 0.65`)** ($0.55\text{–}0.88\text{ air-dry
    tons}$ at $6\text{–}9\%$ moisture). Cultivar architecture strongly governs
    shell yield: paper/soft-shell _Nonpareil_ has a crack-out of $\sim
    65\text{–}70\%$ kernel to in-shell weight ($\sim 0.25\text{–}0.35\text{ dry
    t shell / t kernel}$), semi-hard _Carmel_ and _Independence_ yield $\sim
    0.45\text{–}0.60\text{ dry t/t}$, and hard-shell _Monterey_, _Butte_, and
    _Padre_ yield $\sim 0.70\text{–}0.95\text{ dry t/t}$. Crucially, in the live
    database, `almond shells` (`RF_ID=5`, `factor_type = 'commodity'`) **does**
    produce `12` rows via Path C (`466,374 t` in 2023; `400,554 t` in 2024;
    `466,374 (2023) / 400,554 (2024) t` total) mapped from `primary_ag_product`
    `id=37` (`almond shells`), **but the underlying `county_ag_report_record`
    data is severely corrupted**: in `4` of `6` county-years (San Joaquin 2023
    `110,200 t`, Stanislaus 2023 `235,431 t`, Stanislaus 2024 `241,260 t`, and
    Merced 2023 `119,600 t` vs. `120,743 t`), the County Ag Report ETL copied
    `Almonds, Meats` (`id=1`) tonnage verbatim (`1.000×`) into
    `Almonds, Shells`, and then the duplicate `place` `geoid` join doubled those
    figures again! Switching `RF_ID=5` from `commodity` (`id=37`) to
    `factor_type = 'weight'` (`0.50 / 0.65 / 0.80` bone-dry `t / t kernel`
    against `almonds` `id=1`) bypasses the corrupted `id=37` rows and yields
    verified NSJV totals of **`290,407 dry_tons` (`2023`)** and
    **`297,853 dry_tons` (`2024`)**.
  - **`almond hash` (`id=100`), `almond tree sticks` (`id=108`), &
    `almond stick char` (`id=93`):** During pre-cleaning and gravity-table
    aspiration, hullers separate `almond tree sticks`
    (`0.05–0.15 dry t / t kernel`, twigs swept up with nuts) and `almond hash`
    (`0.020–0.050 dry t / t kernel`, fine kernel chips, testa/skins, and dense
    hull meal that is blended into livestock feed). `almond stick char`
    (`id=93`) is the solid biochar/charcoal (`20–32%` mass yield via slow
    pyrolysis or `8–18%` via downdraft gasification) produced when
    `almond tree sticks` are thermochemically converted.
- **Orchard Woody Biomass (`almond branches` `id=16` vs. `almond woodchips`
  `id=1`):** Long-term UC Cooperative Extension trials in Stanislaus County
  (_Duncan et al., 2019; Doll & Duncan, UC ANR Cost Studies 2021_) proved that
  traditional heavy dormant pruning does not increase cumulative almond yield.
  As a result, most NSJV growers have adopted **minimal pruning** (removing only
  lower limbs that interfere with shakers/sweepers and deadwood), reducing
  annual maintenance pruning yields from the older CBC assumption
  (`1.0–1.5 dry t/ac/yr`) down to **`0.30–0.80 dry t/ac/yr` (`mid = 0.50`)**.
  Conversely, when a $22\text{–}25\text{ year}$ almond orchard is removed at
  end-of-life and chipped into `almond woodchips` (`id=1`), it yields
  **`30–40 bone-dry tons/acre`** ($50\text{–}67\text{ green tons/acre}$ at
  $38\text{–}42\%$ moisture), or **`1.20–1.80 dry t/ac/yr` (`mid = 1.50`)**
  amortized over the orchard's lifespan (_Holtz et al., 2018; Jahanzad et al.,
  2020; Marvinney et al., 2015_). Following the phase-out of open agricultural
  burning by the San Joaquin Valley Air Pollution Control District (SJVAPCD),
  roughly $40\text{–}60\%$ of NSJV orchard removals are now chipped and
  soil-incorporated in situ via **Whole Orchard Recycling (WOR)**.

#### B. English Walnuts (`id=27, 8, 14, 86, 85, 94, 15`)

- **The In-Shell vs. Kernel Denominator Error (`walnut shells` `id=27` &
  `walnut shelling fines` `id=8`):** Unlike almonds (which are reported by
  County Ag Commissioners on a **shelled kernel/meat** basis), California
  English walnuts (_Juglans regia_) are universally reported in County Ag
  Commissioner Reports and USDA NASS on a **dry marketable in-shell basis** at
  $8\%$ moisture (_UC ANR Cost Study for English Walnuts in San Joaquin Valley
  North, Jarvis-Shean et al., 2020_).
  - In `public.residue_factor`, `walnut shells` (`RF_ID=35`) is assigned
    `factor_min = 1.34`, `factor_mid = 1.494`, `factor_max = 1.647`. Those
    numbers are the **shell-to-kernel ratio** ($\frac{1 - K}{K}$) for
    older/harder-shell walnut cultivars with a kernel fraction $K =
    37.8\%\text{–}42.7\%$ ($\frac{0.599}{0.401} = 1.494$).
  - Dominant NSJV cultivars — led by _Chandler_ ($\sim 55\text{–}60\%$ of
    acreage, prized for light-colored halves and thin shells with
    $48\text{–}52\%$ kernel crack-out), _Howard_ ($47\text{–}50\%$ kernel),
    _Tulare_ ($48\text{–}51\%$ kernel), and _Hartley_ ($42\text{–}46\%$ kernel)
    — have an average anatomical shell fraction of **$50\text{–}55\%$ of air-dry
    in-shell weight**, or
    **`0.45–0.53 bone-dry tons of shell per ton of marketed in-shell walnuts`
    (`mid = 0.49`)**.
  - Furthermore, roughly **$20\text{–}25\%$ of California walnuts are exported
    in-shell** (primarily _Hartley_, _Chandler_, and _Howard_ jumbo/large
    grades), meaning those shells leave the country intact. The remaining
    **$75\text{–}80\%$ are cracked at California shelling plants** (such as
    Diamond Foods in Stockton, Mariani Nut in Winters/Modesto, and Poindexter
    Nut), yielding
    **`0.35–0.43 dry tons of captive processor walnut shells per ton of total in-shell walnut production`
    (`mid = 0.39`)**.
  - Worse still, `walnut shelling fines` (`id=8`, `RF_ID=34`) was assigned the
    exact same `1.34 / 1.494 / 1.647` factor as `walnut shells`, generating an
    identical duplicate volume (`334,235 t` in 2023; `241,738 t` in 2024) in
    `mv_biomass_volume_estimate`! In walnut cracking plants, shelling fines
    (crushed shell dust $<1\text{–}2\text{ mm}$, flaky internal woody
    septum/packing tissue, and kernel skin dust removed by air aspiration)
    represent only **$3\text{–}8\%$ of shell weight**, or
    **`0.015–0.045 dry tons per ton of in-shell walnuts` (`mid = 0.025`)**.
- **Green Walnut Hulling (`walnut hulls` `id=14` & `walnut hulling discharge`
  `id=86`):** Walnuts are harvested when the green fleshy pericarp (hull)
  dehisces ($82\text{–}87\%$ moisture). At on-farm or central
  huller/dehydrators, mechanical abrasive brushes and water sprays strip the
  green hull before the in-shell nuts are dried from $\sim 20\text{–}30\%$ down
  to $8\%$ moisture in forced-air bins at $43^\circ\text{C}$
  ($110^\circ\text{F}$). On a bone-dry basis, green walnut hulls represent
  **`0.20–0.30 dry tons per ton of dry marketable in-shell walnuts`
  (`mid = 0.25`)** (_Thompson et al., 1998; Khir et al., 2013_). Live DB
  `RF_ID=33` uses `0.524`, overestimating dry walnut hulls by $2.1\times$.

#### C. Pistachios (`id=10, 35, 24`)

- **Reconciling the Wet Field-Run vs. Dry Marketable In-Shell Mass Balance:** As
  established in our earlier pistachio investigation, California pistachio
  hullers receive **raw wet unhulled field-run clusters** (_Kerman_, _Golden
  Hills_, _Lost Hills_) directly from catch-frame harvesters. According to the
  **American Pistachio Growers (APG)**, **Administrative Committee for
  Pistachios (ACP)**, **USDA NASS _Noncitrus Fruits and Nuts_ (2018–2024)**, and
  **UC ANR Publications (_Ferguson & Haviland, 2016_, Pub 3545; _Ferguson et
  al., 2020_; _Venkitasamy et al., 2017_)**, it takes **$3.00\text{–}3.25\text{
  lb}$ of raw wet field-run weight at the huller receiving scale to yield
  $1.00\text{ lb}$ of dry marketable in-shell pistachios** at $5\text{–}7\%$
  moisture:
  - **100 lb Raw Wet Field-Run Delivery** ($= 32.1\text{ lb}$ dry marketable
    in-shell nuts):
    1. **Receiving Trash (`pistachio stems & leaves`, `id=24`):**
       $6\text{–}10\text{ lb}$ wet rachis stems, leaves, twigs, and
       floaters/blanks ($45\text{–}55\%$ MC) $\approx 3.2\text{ lb}$ bone-dry
       ($\implies \frac{3.2}{32.1} = \mathbf{0.10\text{ dry t / t dry
       in-shell}}$, whereas $\frac{1.8\text{–}3.2}{32.1}$ on wet receiving basis
       is `0.056` in live DB, and wet trash/blanks is `0.17–0.50` in the
       sheet!).
    2. **Wet Green Pericarp Hull (`pistachio hulls`, `id=35`):**
       $52\text{–}58\text{ lb}$ wet fleshy hull before wash water
       ($72\text{–}75\%$ MC) $\approx 15.2\text{ lb}$ bone-dry ($\implies
       \frac{15.2}{32.1} = \mathbf{0.47\text{ dry t / t dry in-shell}}$, whereas
       $\frac{15.2}{103.2} = \mathbf{0.147}$ per ton of _wet field-run weight_
       in the live DB, and $\frac{55}{32.1} = \mathbf{1.71\text{ wet t / t}}$ in
       the sheet `1.155–2.0`!).
    3. **Wet Hulled In-Shell Nuts:** $36\text{–}40\text{ lb}$ wet in-shell nuts
       at $35\text{–}40\%$ moisture, dried in rotary/vertical dryers and
       tempering silos to **$32.1\text{ lb}$ dry marketable in-shell
       pistachios** ($5\text{–}7\%$ MC).
  - **Anatomical vs. Captive Processor Shell (`pistachio shells`, `id=10`):**
    Within $1.00\text{ ton}$ of dry marketable in-shell pistachios, the
    anatomical shell comprises $48\text{–}51\%$ of dry weight
    (**`0.45–0.50 bone-dry t / t in-shell`, `mid = 0.48`**; _Kashaninejad et
    al., 2006_). However, because pistachio shells naturally split along their
    suture on the tree (`75–85%` open-mouth in-shell market share in **USDA NASS
    _Noncitrus Fruits and Nuts_ 2018–2024**), **$75\text{–}80\%$ of California's
    crop is roasted, salted, and sold to consumers in-shell**, meaning those
    shells are discarded in consumer households worldwide rather than at the
    processor. Only the **$20\text{–}25\%$ closed-shell, stained, or small
    shelling stock** is mechanically cracked at California processing plants,
    generating
    **`0.10–0.13 dry tons of captive processor pistachio shells per ton of total dry in-shell production`
    (`mid = 0.12`)**.

### 4.2 Deep-Dive Technical Analysis — Batch 2

#### A. Vineyards & Winemaking in NSJV Crush Districts 11 & 12 (`id=22, 124, 125, 23, 7, 4, 37, 20, 25, 92, 13, 91, 114`)

- **Scale & Structure of NSJV Winemaking (_CDFA California Grape Crush Report,
  2023/2024; Jin et al., 2021_):** San Joaquin County (Crush District 11 —
  Lodi/Woodbridge, $\sim 95,000\text{ bearing acres}$ and $\sim
  650,000\text{–}800,000\text{ tons/yr}$ crushed) and Stanislaus/Merced counties
  (Crush District 12, $\sim 35,000\text{ bearing acres}$, home to the world's
  largest wineries including E. & J. Gallo Winery in Modesto and Livingston and
  Bronco Wine Company in Ceres) process over **$1.2\text{ million wet tons of
  wine grapes annually}$** (including inter-district shipments from District 13
  in Madera/Fresno).
- **`grape pomace` (`id=22`) Wet-vs-Dry Discrepancy:** When fresh grapes
  ($75\text{–}82\%$ moisture, $20\text{–}26^\circ\text{Brix}$) are crushed and
  pressed in pneumatic bladder or continuous screw presses, **wet grape pomace**
  (consisting of $\sim 45\text{–}55\%$ skins/cuticle, $\sim 35\text{–}45\%$
  seeds, and $\sim 5\text{–}15\%$ residual pulp/stem fragments) accounts for
  **$14\text{–}25\%$ of initial fresh grape weight
  (`0.14–0.25 wet t / wet t crushed`)** at **$55\text{–}68\%$ moisture**
  (_ustos/UC Davis Viticulture & Enology; Muhlack et al., 2018; Dwyer et al.,
  2014; Williams et al., 2015_).
  - **White Grape Pomace (`id=124`):** Pressed _immediately after crushing_
    before alcoholic fermentation (_Chardonnay_, _Pinot Gris_, _Sauvignon
    Blanc_, _French Colombard_). Because soluble sugars and pectins remain in
    the skins/pulp, wet yield is higher (`0.16–0.25 wet t/t`) and wetter
    (`62–70% MC`), yielding **`0.055–0.100 bone-dry tons per wet ton crushed`
    (`mid = 0.075`)**.
  - **Red Grape Pomace (`id=22`):** Fermented on the skins and pressed _after_
    alcoholic fermentation (_Cabernet Sauvignon_, _Zinfandel_, _Merlot_, _Petite
    Sirah_, _Rubired_). Sugar has been converted to ethanol and extracted,
    yielding a drier press cake (`0.12–0.20 wet t/t` at `52–60% MC`) equal to
    **`0.050–0.110 bone-dry tons per wet ton crushed` (`mid = 0.080`)**.
  - **The Database Bug:** The Google Sheet was updated to the correct bone-dry
    range (`0.05 / 0.08 / 0.11`), but the live PostgreSQL database (`RF_ID=12`)
    still holds the **wet** factor (`0.20 / 0.25 / 0.30`), inflating NSJV dry
    grape pomace in `mv_biomass_volume_estimate` from `130,714 dry_tons` to
    `408,481.5 dry_tons` ($3.12\times$ overestimate).
- **`grape stem` (`id=23`) vs. `grape vines` (`id=7`) Copy-Paste Error:** In
  winemaking terminology, `grape stem` refers to the green/woody **peduncle and
  rachis (cluster stem)** separated from the berries by rotary cage destemmers
  at the winery receiving hopper. For whole-cluster hand-harvested grapes, wet
  rachis represents $3\text{–}6\%$ of fresh cluster weight
  (`0.030–0.060 wet t/t` at `65–75% MC`, or
  **`0.008–0.018 dry t / wet t crushed`**). Moreover, in Crush Districts 11 and
  12, **$>85\text{–}90\%$ of wine grapes are mechanically harvested** by
  trunk/canopy-shaking straddle harvesters equipped with on-board destemmers and
  MOG (Material Other than Grape) blowers, which **leave the rachis attached to
  the cordon or blow it directly onto the vineyard floor**, reducing
  winery-received `grape stem` to **`0.002–0.006 dry t / wet t crushed`
  (`mid = 0.004`)**! In `public.resource_availability` (`RA_ID=26`) and
  `resource_info.json`, `grape stem` (`id=23`, categorized as
  `"Processing Waste"`) was accidentally assigned `2.0 wet t/ac`, `45% MC`,
  `1.1 dry t/ac` — the exact dormant woody pruning yield of `grape vines`
  (`id=7`, `RA_ID=28`)! As a result, `mv_usda_county_production` outputs two
  identical rows of `204,807.9 dry_tons` in the NSJV (`grape stem` and
  `grape vines`), double-counting vineyard prunings and overestimating winery
  cluster stems by $\sim 25\times$.

#### B. Olives (`id=30, 126, 17, 106, 95`)

- **2-Phase vs. 3-Phase Olive Oil Extraction & Table Olive Canning (_UC Olive
  Center; Flynn et al., 2014; Christoforou & Fokaides, 2016_):** California's
  olive industry is bifurcated into two completely different processing chains,
  both heavily represented in the NSJV:
  1. **Super-High-Density (SHD) Olive Oil Milling (~55–60% of CA tonnage):**
     Concentrated in San Joaquin and Stanislaus counties (e.g., Corto Olive in
     Lodi, Cobram Estate, California Olive Ranch partnerships), growing
     _Arbequina_, _Arbosana_, and _Koroneiki_ at $600\text{–}900\text{
     trees/acre}$. Modern California mills exclusively use **2-phase horizontal
     decanter centrifuges** (which require minimal added water and eliminate
     liquid olive mill wastewater [OMWW / _alpechín_]). A 2-phase mill
     discharges
     **`0.75–0.85 wet tons of wet pomace (*alperujo*) per wet ton of milled olives`**
     at **`60–70% moisture`**, containing crushed skin, pulp, vegetative water,
     and fractured stone (`0.24–0.32 bone-dry t / wet t oil olives`,
     `mid = 0.28`). Older **3-phase mills** generate a drier press cake
     (_orujo_, `0.45–0.55 wet t/t` at `45–55% MC` = `0.22–0.28 dry t/t`) plus a
     separate liquid OMWW stream.
  2. **Table Olive Canning (~40–45% of CA tonnage):** Centered at **Musco Family
     Olive Co. in Tracy (San Joaquin County)** and **Bell-Carter Foods
     (Modesto/Corning)**, processing _Manzanillo_ and _Sevillano_ cultivars into
     California black-ripe canned olives. Table olives are **never crushed into
     pomace**! Instead, they are lye-cured, oxidized, and mechanically pitted,
     generating **`olive pits` (`id=126`)** at
     `0.14–0.20 wet t / t pitted olives` at `28–35% MC`
     (**`0.09–0.13 dry t / wet t table olives`, `mid = 0.11`**).

## 5. Batch 3 Audit: Stone Fruit & Pome Fruit Processing & Orchard Streams (45 Resources)

### 5.1 Origins of the 42-Row Stone/Pome Processing Matrix & Git Commit `6b575b17`

Why does `ca-biositing` contain an identical 7-stream template (`peels`,
`pomace`, `seeds`, `stems / leaves`, `rejects`, `screening waste`,
`off-spec canned products`) across **6 fruits** (`peach`, `apricot`,
`nectarine`, `plum`, `pluot`, `pear` = 42 rows + `nectarine pit` +
`peach prunings` + `pruning residues, noncitrus` = 45 resources), and why are 41
of the 42 processing rows currently `NULL` while `apricot stems / leaves`
(`id=47`) is `1.5 dry t/ac`?

1. **The NSJV Fruit Cannery Survey Template:** During project initialization,
   the database catalog was populated from a standardized processor waste
   questionnaire designed for **North San Joaquin Valley multi-fruit canneries**
   — specifically **Del Monte Foods (Modesto)**, **Pacific Coast Producers (Lodi
   & Oroville)**, and **Seneca Foods (Modesto, closed 2020)**. These facilities
   pack canned **Clingstone Peaches**, **Apricots**, **Bartlett Pears**, and
   **Fruit Cocktail**, where unit operations sequentially generate:
   - `stems / leaves`: Bin-dumping, air-blowing, and water-flume trash removal
     at receiving.
   - `rejects`: Optical/manual sorting culls (undersized, split-pit, green,
     bruised, insect/fungal damaged fruit).
   - `seeds`: Torque/spoon pitting of peaches and apricots (`pits`), or
     mechanical coring/stemming of pears (`cores`).
   - `peels`: Hot caustic ($\text{KOH}/\text{NaOH}$) or high-pressure steam
     peeling followed by rotary wash reels.
   - `screening waste`: Rotary wedge-wire / vibrating screen solids (fleshy
     fragments, dicing crumbs, slicer trimmings) recovered from plant wastewater
     before municipal/land discharge.
   - `pomace`: Finisher/pulper press cake (fibrous pulp, skins, stone cells)
     from fruit puree, nectar, baby food, and juice concentrate lines (where
     grade-B fruit, sort-outs, and dicing fragments are pureed).
   - `off-spec canned products`: Dented, under-vacuum, seam-defective, or
     QA-rejected cans sent to mechanical de-canners.
2. **The Agronomic & Commercial Reality (Processed vs. Fresh-Market Stone
   Fruit):** When this 7-stream cannery template was Cartesian-multiplied across
   all 6 stone/pome fruits in the catalog, it ignored a fundamental bifurcation
   of California pomology:
   - **Commercially Canned/Processed in NSJV (`Clingstone Peaches`, `Apricots`,
     `Bartlett Pears`, `Dried Plums / Prunes`):** Over $95\%$ of California
     Clingstone Peaches (grown heavily in Stanislaus and Merced counties), $\sim
     65\text{–}75\%$ of Apricots (Stanislaus and San Joaquin counties lead the
     state), $\sim 70\text{–}80\%$ of Bartlett Pears (Sacramento River Delta /
     San Joaquin County), and $\sim 100\%$ of Dried Plums (_French_ prune)
     undergo industrial canning, pureeing, or dehydration/pitting.
   - **100% Fresh-Market Crops (`Nectarines`, `Pluots`,
     `Fresh-Market Japanese Plums`):** Grown primarily in the South San Joaquin
     Valley (Fresno, Tulare, Kings counties), **Nectarines, Pluots, and Japanese
     Plums (_Prunus salicina_) are NOT commercially canned, peeled, or pitted in

     `pomace`, `screening waste`, and `off-spec canned products` are **`0.00`
     (non-existent)** except when tiny volumes of packinghouse culls are
     diverted to specialty cold-pressed juice/puree processors.

### 6.2 Deep-Dive Technical Analysis — Batch 4

#### A. The NSJV Corn Stover Silage vs. Grain Bug (`id=12, 26, 21, 33, 119`)

This is the single largest numerical distortion in the entire `ca-biositing`
platform (`9,932,409 dry_tons` across the four corn stover streams in the NSJV):

1. **Agronomic Reality of Corn in the North San Joaquin Valley (_UC ANR Corn
   Silage Cost Study 2023; Heguy et al., UCCE Stanislaus/Merced/San Joaquin_):**
   Merced, Stanislaus, and San Joaquin counties house roughly **550,000
   lactating dairy cows** (over one-third of California's dairy herd). To feed
   these cows, NSJV farmers plant **$150,000\text{–}180,000\text{ acres/year}$
   of corn**, of which **$>85\text{–}90\%$ is harvested as whole-plant Corn
   Silage** (`CORN, SILAGE`) at $65\text{–}70\%$ moisture ($30\text{–}35\%$ dry
   matter), yielding $25\text{–}30\text{ wet tons/acre}$. During silage harvest,
   self-propelled forage harvesters cut the corn stalk $4\text{–}6\text{
   inches}$ above the ground and chop the **entire above-ground plant — stalks,
   leaves, husks, cobs, and developing kernels — directly into silage trucks**
   for anaerobic ensiling at the dairy. **Harvestable field stover from corn
   silage acreage is strictly $0.00\text{ tons/acre}$.**
2. **How Path E (`census_production_based`) Confuses Wet Silage Tons with Dry
   Grain Tons:** In USDA NASS QuickStats (`public.observation`), corn production
   is split into two distinct commodities with different canonical units:
   - `CORN, GRAIN - PRODUCTION, MEASURED IN BU`: Across our NSJV dataset
     (`22,136` total grain acres across 6 county-year observations, primarily on
     peat soils of the San Joaquin River Delta), grain production is
     **$3,603,803\text{ bushels}$**, which at $56\text{ lb/bu}$ equals
     **$100,906\text{ air-dry tons of grain}$** ($85,266\text{ bone-dry tons}$
     at $15.5\%$ moisture, or $\sim 14,200\text{ bone-dry tons/yr}$ across the 3
     counties).
   - `CORN, SILAGE - PRODUCTION, MEASURED IN TONS`: Across the same NSJV dataset
     (`164,550` silage acres), wet silage production is **$20,649,341\text{ wet
     tons}$**. Because
     [`mv_biomass_volume_estimate.py` (line 383)](file:///Users/pjsmitty301/ca-biositing/src/ca_biositing/datamodels/ca_biositing/datamodels/data_portal_views/mv_biomass_volume_estimate.py#L383)
     filters `Unit.name == 'tons'`, it completely ignores `CORN, GRAIN` (`bu`)
     and instead multiplies **$20.65\text{ million wet tons of whole-plant dairy
     silage}$** by the dry grain-to-stover harvest index (`1.02` whole, `0.571`
     stalks, `0.153` cobs, `0.082` husks)!
3. **Anatomical Partitioning & Missing Leaf Fraction (_Pordesimo et al., 2004;
   Shinners & Binford, 2006_):** Look closely at the project's four corn stover
   factors:
   - `corn stover whole` (`id=12`): `mid = 1.02`
   - `corn stover stalks` (`id=26`): `mid = 0.571` ($= 0.560 \times 1.02$)
   - `corn stover cobs` (`id=21`): `mid = 0.153` ($= 0.150 \times 1.02$)
   - `corn stover husks` (`id=33`): `mid = 0.082` ($= 0.080 \times 1.02$) Notice
     that $0.560 + 0.150 + 0.080 = 0.790$ ($0.806 / 1.02$). The project derived
     its component factors from the classic USDA/ORNL physiological partitioning
     of corn stover at physiological maturity (**$56\%$ stalks, $21\%$
     leaves/blades, $15\%$ cobs, $8\%$ husks**; _Pordesimo et al., 2004_),
     omitting the $21\%$ leaf fraction (`0.214`) while simultaneously including
     both the whole stover (`1.02`) and its three sub-fractions in
     `mv_biomass_volume_estimate` (double-counting by $1.79\times$).
     Furthermore, `1.02` is the ratio of **dry stover to dry grain** (assuming a
     $1:1$ dry grain-to-stover ratio adjusted by $2\%$), whereas marketed grain
     is at $15.5\%$ moisture (so per ton of air-dry marketed grain, bone-dry
     gross stover is $1.00 \times (1 - 0.155) = \mathbf{0.845\text{–}0.92\text{
     dry t / t air-dry grain}}$, and sustainably collectible stover at
     $40\text{–}50\%$ removal is **`0.35–0.45 dry t / t air-dry grain`** or
     **`1.5–2.3 dry t/ac`**).

#### B. Processing Tomatoes (`tomato pomace`, `id=19`)

California produces $>90\%$ of U.S. processing tomatoes ($10.5\text{–}12.0\text{
million wet tons/yr}$ statewide), with the NSJV (Merced, San Joaquin, and
Stanislaus counties) harvesting **$>1.7\text{ million wet tons/year}$** and
hosting major paste canneries including **Ingomar Packing Co.** and **The
Morning Star Packing Co.** (Los Banos, Merced County), **Stanislaus Food
Products** (Modesto), **ConAgra Brands** (Oakdale), and **Escalon Premier
Brands** (Escalon).

- During hot-break ($85\text{–}95^\circ\text{C}$) or cold-break
  ($60\text{–}66^\circ\text{C}$) pulping and finishing, fresh paste tomatoes
  ($94.2\text{–}94.8\%$ moisture) pass through $0.5\text{–}1.5\text{ mm}$ rotary
  screens. The rejected **wet tomato pomace** (comprising $\sim 45\text{–}55\%$
  seeds, $\sim 40\text{–}50\%$ skin/cuticle, and fibrous pulp on a dry basis)
  represents **$2.5\text{–}5.0\%$ of incoming fresh tomato weight
  (`0.025–0.050 wet t / wet t`, `mid = 0.038–0.040`)** (_Del Valle et al., 2006;
  Pan et al., 2015; Williams et al., 2015; Jin et al., 2020_).
- Crucially, wet tomato pomace exits the finisher press at **$68\text{–}80\%$
  moisture (average $73\%$ MC, $27\%$ dry matter)**. Therefore, the bone-dry
  yield per wet ton of fresh tomatoes is:
  $$\text{Dry Tomato Pomace Factor} = (0.025\text{ to }0.050\text{ wet t/t}) \times (1 - 0.73) = \mathbf{0.007\text{ to }0.014\text{ dry t / wet t fresh tomatoes}}\quad (\mathbf{\text{mid} = 0.010})$$
- Because `RF_ID=31` uses `0.03 / 0.04 / 0.05` (the _wet_ pomace ratio) and
  `mv_biomass_volume_estimate` labels the output `dry_tons`, NSJV dry tomato
  pomace is overestimated by **$4.0\times$**
  (`116,681.16 (2023) / 90,838.20 (2024).4 dry_tons` instead of
  `~51,880 dry_tons`).

#### C. Merced County Sweet Potatoes (`sweet potato culls`, `id=28`)

Merced County (centered on the sandy Atwater, Livingston, and Ballico soils) is
the heart of the western U.S. sweet potato industry, harvesting
**$18,000\text{–}22,000\text{ acres/year}$** and **$350,000\text{–}400,000\text{
wet tons/year}$** ($\sim 90\%$ of California's crop; _Stoddard et al., UC ANR
Sample Costs to Produce Sweetpotatoes in San Joaquin Valley North, 2021_).

- In the UC ANR Merced County Cost Study (_Stoddard et al., 2021_), field-run
  harvest yields average $\sim 37,500\text{ lb/ac}$ ($18.75\text{ wet t/ac}$),
  packed into three marketable commercial box grades: **U.S. No. 1** ($\sim
  55\text{–}60\%$ of packed weight), **Mediums / Canner** ($\sim
  20\text{–}25\%$), and **Jumbos** ($\sim 15\text{–}20\%$), while **field and
  packinghouse culls** (rotten, wireworm/grub damaged, severely misshapen, or
  broken roots removed during storage curing and water-flume washing) account
  for **$10\text{–}20\%$ of total harvested weight
  (`mid = 0.15 wet t / wet t`)**.
- Why did `RF_ID=30` enter `0.312 / 0.406 / 0.50`? The author of that entry
  treated everything outside the premium U.S. No. 1 grade ($\sim 40.6\%$
  Mediums + Jumbos + culls) as "culls," even though Mediums and Jumbos are
  commercially marketed for retail, foodservice, sweet potato fries, and puree!
- Even more importantly, fresh sweet potato storage roots (_Covington_, _Diane_,
  _Beauregard_, _O'Henry_) contain **$72\text{–}75\%$ water ($25\text{–}28\%$
  dry matter, avg $27\%$ DM)**. Multiplying wet harvested tonnage by `0.406`
  without multiplying by $0.27$ dry matter content inflates bone-dry sweet
  potato culls (`0.027 / 0.041 / 0.054 dry t / wet t` for true culls, or
  `0.110 dry t / wet t` if Medium/Jumbo grades are included) by
  **$7.4\times\text{ to }9.9\times$**.
